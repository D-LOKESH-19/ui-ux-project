# 3. Personas and Empathy Map

*Stage: Define. These are proto-personas built from the literature, not from interviews. Each attribute is tagged with its source. Validate them with real users in future work.*

## Persona 1: Aarav, the night-worried student

- 21, engineering student, type 1 diabetes for 8 years, wears a CGM and a smartwatch.
- **Goal:** sleep through the night while staying safe from lows [4].
- **Frustrations:** night alerts are missed or wake him roughly [4][5]; alarms in class are disruptive [1].
- **Behaviour:** has silenced some alarms to cope, which worries him [1].
- **Needs:** a gentle but reliable night alert that confirms he responded, with a caregiver backup [2].

## Persona 2: Meera, the active runner

- 28, IT professional, type 1 diabetes, runs and does gym three times a week.
- **Goal:** exercise without fear of a low [7].
- **Frustrations:** cannot read small text while running; worries the sensor number lags reality [6][10].
- **Needs:** giant digits, trend arrow, a predictive warning, honest signal-lost states [3][7].

## Persona 3: Mr. Suresh Rao, the older adult

- 67, retired, insulin-treated diabetes, wears a CGM, lives with his spouse.
- **Goal:** know "am I okay?" in one look without reading small text.
- **Frustrations:** small text, glare at night, too many beeps [1].
- **Needs:** very large numbers, plain words, big buttons, soft colours, and a spouse as caregiver backup [2].
- *Design assumption: older adults often have reduced vision and dexterity. Confirm with a cited source or user testing.*

## Empathy map: says, thinks, does, feels

| | Content |
|---|---|
| Says | "Too many beeps." "I can't hear it at night." "Is this number even right?" |
| Thinks | "If I ignore this one I might miss the real one." "Will I go low mid-run?" |
| Does | Changes alarm settings, silences alerts, confirms with a fingerstick [1][2][10] |
| Feels | Annoyed, anxious, distrustful of the device, afraid of hypoglycemia [1][7] |

*The phrases in the Says row are illustrative paraphrases of the research findings in Section 2, not recorded participant statements.*

## Pain points and unmet needs

| Pain point | Unmet need |
|---|---|
| All alarms feel identical | Clear alert levels |
| Alerts missed at night | Escalating, confirmed alerts |
| Screen unreadable in exercise | Huge, minimal display and a hardware-button option |
| Sensor lag and signal loss | Visible data age and confidence |
| Alarm overload | Predictive, grouped alerts with smart snooze |
| Small text and glare | Large plain text; soft day and night themes |

## Problem reframed as a design question

**How might we help a person with diabetes, including an older adult, know the right thing at the right urgency, with one glance or one buzz, whether they are running, sitting or asleep?**
